POWER SLAP IS REAL

DAMIEN DIBBELL · BEYOND THE ROUNDS

By JK47

Share on XShare on Facebook

People love to say Power Slap isn't a sport. Sports editors call it dumb. It feels like no sane person would enjoy it, let alone want to compete in it. Until you listen to Damien Dibbell talk to my dawg for 37 minutes.

Before he was the heavyweight champion, Damien was just a nerdy kid. His words: "I was always the smart quiet kid just off in the corner doing my thing." He reads. He loves anime. He played soccer growing up, and his parents wouldn't let him play football because he was too smart to be messing up his head. Years later, when he signed with Power Slap, all they said was "you a grown man, go do your thing." His dad was in the military, and the plan was the military, the GI Bill, then law school. But the quiet kid had always been drawn to combat sports.

Coming out of school, he was trying to get a couple of amateur boxing fights. Then he saw Slap Fighting, years before Power Slap existed, in bars and warehouses. He thought it looked fun and he likes to box in the pocket, stand and bang, so he signed up. He did it for about two years with Slap Fight Championships before Power Slap ever sent him a DM on Instagram.

That's what gets me. Nobody pushed him into this. A smart kid with a law school plan chose to stand at a podium and take a slap from a grown man because he thought it was cool. That takes more guts than typing that it's not a sport. And it didn't cost him his other dreams. It opened doors to them, with pro boxing offers and an easier road to law school.

And he was a fan first. When SDawg brought up Ron "Wolverine" Bata, Damien lit up. He'd watched Wolverine's war with Darius back in the underground days, a 27-round match. Then Damien had to face the guy he used to watch, for the belt at Power Slap 2. He dropped Wolverine in round four and won the title. He's careful with how he says it: someone else was the first to knock Wolverine out, but he was the first to drop him. That's not bragging. That's a fan who knows the history and knows exactly where his name fits in it.

SDawg and Damien go back and forth like two guys at a bar who've watched every event. When SDawg told him everyone says Wolverine hits the hardest, Damien smiled and said "you've been talking to the wrong people because everyone I fought has said I hit the hardest." Asked how you line up a slap with that legendary beard on Wolverine, he said you have to caress the face first lol. "It's a real sensual movement," he chuckles. Damien is so relatable. His favorite part of being in the main event is getting the nicest hotel room.

He trains like a scientist. He trains weighted, in socks on a hardwood floor, so his foot doesn't slip. He uses a fifteen pound weight for speed. Different weights for different techniques. These details in training and perspective aren't for showmanship. That's a man reflecting, testing, and perfecting his craft.

This interview is almost 2 years old, link in the replies. Back then Damien called his first fight with Dorian way too close and said he wouldn't be mad running it back. He got that rematch at Power Slap 22 in July and won again, still close, by majority decision. He knocked out Darius in April. Today he's 8-0 in Power Slap, heavyweight champion since May 2023, with six title defenses. Nobody else in the promotion has that many.

Hat tip to SDawg, my dawg. He did his homework, asked real questions, and you can tell he's a fan of the sport, not just the clips. I hung out on his Power Slap 25 watch-along and had a blast.

Call it what you want. The smart quiet kid found it on his own, took it serious, and nobody's beaten him yet. That's not crazy. That's an athlete.
