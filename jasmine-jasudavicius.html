JASMINE JASUDAVICIUS

UFC EDMONTON · BEYOND THE ROUNDS

By JK47

She went for fireworks and ended up in a cage in front of 19,000 people.

Her friend asked. Jasmine Jasudavicius didn't ask why. Maybe the why isn't what really matters in life. She said yes to the fireworks. She didn't know the guy tagging along on her friend's date would become her partner and coach. She said yes to his trip. She didn't know she'd watch fighters train, or that she'd want in.

Sitting at a bar in Cuba, wrestling in the sand had never crossed her mind. But she said yes. They smashed her face into the beach over and over. Somehow a mouth full of sand made her smile. Not because it led somewhere. Just to feel it. Just to learn something.

She was 26 and had never thrown a punch. Too late to chase a belt? She wasn't chasing one. Somehow she's close to one anyway. There was no plan. No promise at the other end. No destination either, just cruising. Every gym has guys walk in on day one and announce they're going to be UFC champion. They're chasing glory, not the art. Jas never made that speech. She just kept saying yes.

Yes to walking through a restaurant kitchen to train on mats where the fryer oil leaked down from the ceiling. Yes to a basement under a reptile shop, stepping on crickets every morning. People think every UFC fighter is rich. The job must be glorious. She was training under a reptile shop.

She probably wasn't showing the crickets on social media for attention. She was just going through what she had to go through. In the moment it's a struggle. Years later it's funny. Then it's pride. It never feels like "the journey" while you're in the middle of it. The people selling glory usually aren't the ones paving a road for anyone. And making a path isn't always tearing down a forest. Sometimes you just walk into the tall grass. Every step forward folds it down. Others may or may not see the trail from far away.

This week she got fined $10K for "dip." It wasn't even dip. Still leaves the question: why have anything in your mouth when you're about to fight? Same reason as the fireworks, the trip, the sand. She didn't need one. She's just living. She never set out to be a story, but we're writing it.

Saturday in Edmonton, #4 Erin Blanchfield fights #6 Jasmine Jasudavicius. Real name, no gimmicks.
