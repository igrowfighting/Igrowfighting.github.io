JOE KROPSCHOT

UFC VEGAS 123 · BEYOND THE ROUNDS

By JK47

Joe Kropschot was the violent kid his parents couldn't line out.

Suspended from school 10 to 20 times for fights. Weapons getting pulled. Police getting called. He was a sophomore going down a bad path with the wrong crew, and his parents ran out of answers. So they had him kidnapped out of his own house and taken to Utah, a state with easier laws for programs like that.

He was 15.

He spent 11 months out there. For 3 of them he hiked every day and lived out of a backpack, with nothing but what he could carry. The program was dangerous and later got shut down. There were 8 kids there. Only 2 are still here today, and Joe is one of them. The other 6 are dead from overdose and suicide.

He was already a fighter in the streets, and he'd grown up watching Anderson Silva. Out in that desert, Joe decided he was going to be a UFC fighter. One of the other kids believed him. "I think you could do it, bro. You're tough." Joe had no reason to believe he could turn his life around, but he held onto it. That's what got him through Utah.

At 16 he came home and met Victoria. He told her he was going to be a UFC fighter. Most people would laugh at a kid fresh out of a program saying that. She believed him from day one.

He found a gym, and the gym is what saved him. Not one coach, a whole tribe. Guys to train with, struggle with, and make each other better. All that anger finally had somewhere to go. He's been a coach and trainer for the last 6 years now, and he doesn't care about the cars or the nice things. He just wants to train and be around his family.

Even that wish still wasn't easy. He started his pro career 4 and 0, then lost 3 straight. He dusted himself off again and won his next 4. That made him 8 and 3, and 6 of those wins came by submission.

Then his dad passed away, and his life flipped upside down. Two days later he got the call for the Contender Series. It was hard to celebrate. But the fight gave him something to focus on again, something to put everything into.

He won, took the 0 off a man who was 10 and 0, and broke the guy's arm in the process. He wouldn't tap, and his elbow bent the other direction. After the fight, with his forehead swollen and marked up, Joe dedicated the win to his dad. He knows he was watching.

The desert couldn't stop him at 15. Saturday The Land Animal prowls back into another one. This time with the Las Vegas lights and a cage fight, for his UFC debut.
