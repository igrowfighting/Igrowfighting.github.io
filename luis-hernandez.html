LUIS HERNANDEZ

UFC VEGAS 121 · BEYOND THE ROUNDS

By JK47

Luis Hernandez just debuted at #UFCVegas121 and stormed through Sedriques Dumas in 57 seconds. Standing guillotine. Locked it from mount, stood up with it still on, just because he could. It didn’t make it easier. Then he got a finish that looked like a statement. Then they tied a black belt on him in the cage.

He has the physique of a pro wrestler, and when the camera runs he doesn’t just look the part. He is the part. I have no doubt Luis isn’t throwing D-Generation X suck-it signs while he’s patrolling the streets of Miami. He knows when to do it. There’s something special about knowing when to turn it up loud and when to turn it down low. That’s something fighters are picking up on. Sometimes it’s cringe. Sometimes you can’t look away. Maybe Luis shouldn't of done the gesture when he defeated one of Strickland's guys. Maybe he should of. More and more new guys are walking into the Apex with a persona built to make a spectacle out of a card a lot of fans still mock as no-names. One week it’s a silhouette of Michael Jackson. The next it’s a mustache with dance moves that look like they should be named after Hostess cakes — the Ding Dong and the Ho Ho.

That’s a Miami-Dade deputy. First active one in the UFC. Dumas is a convicted felon with a long arrest sheet and a sentence from this summer. The promotion sold cop vs convict. They didn’t shy away from a topic of controversy a lot of professional analysts would never touch. I’ve seen this storyline before — fighters’ jobs, fighters’ records, the whole contrast sitting right there in the room — and the booth usually keeps it half said. Saturday they didn’t. Maybe that’s what happens when the pro wrestling side and the fight side keep brushing elbows under the same TKO umbrella. Whatever makes it rain. Both guys seemed fine playing in it. Eleven days after Hernandez won a contract on Contender Series, he took the short-notice spot and they walked in like they had an agreement on the roles.

Paramount’s booth leaned all the way in. Jokes about Dumas using his weapons on police. Jokes about Hernandez tasing him on the takedown. I’m not mad. It was entertaining. It just sounded more like a timeline argument than old-school commentary, and that’s the part that caught me. Comfort levels move. What a booth will say out loud moves with them. I can’t help wondering if the Apex makes that easier — less crowd, less eyes, a little more room to kick back and be themselves.

The 11 days is the part people should sit with. Short notice is not illegal. Damage is. A lot of commissions will sit you 30 days, sometimes longer, if the last fight left a mark. Nevada’s written rest for MMA is 4 days per round of your last fight unless they give permission. His last one was 2 rounds on September 15. That puts him on 8 days on paper. This fight was 11 days later, same building he just got licensed and medically cleared in. That’s why it happened. If Guillon had cracked him worse, or if this card was in a stricter state, he is watching from the couch.

But Luis didn’t watch. He came in on short notice, stood up in a choke for fun, and left 9-0. Dumas took the booking. The product was the product. I got what I paid for and I can’t stop thinking about how normal the jokes sounded.

I respected both of them for taking the fight and fighting it. I enjoyed the product. That’s the whole job of Saturday night, and they did it.

Well played, gentlemen. Bravo.
