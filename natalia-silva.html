NATALIA SILVA

UFC 332 · BEYOND THE ROUNDS

By JK47

Most people in America who know Natalia Silva know the kick: a spinning back kick to the chin as a takedown came in, a late '22 highlight. That was not the beginning of her story. Since that night she has beaten former champions and fought through the kind of loss a gym can't fix. The streak is fourteen. The next name on the sheet was Valentina Shevchenko. That is the part where the rankings finally caught up to a girl from a town nobody was scouting.

She grew up in Pingo-d'Água, Minas Gerais. She calls it a little town. About five thousand people. "Like one street in Belo Horizonte," she said. For us Americans, that's one block in Atlanta. At 16 she joined a free community taekwondo class under Professor Ney. She never left. Two years later — not even knowing what MMA was — she accepted a fight. No team. No MMA training. Twenty days out, not knowing how to cut weight, she stopped eating and dropped thirteen pounds. She lost. People mocked her. Her mother and five siblings were scared, but still stood with her. She sold raffle tickets and ran bingo to make those early cards.

Then she got to Contagem. Carlos Borracha (head coach at Team Borracha, Paulo Costa's brother) saw her submit somebody at a show he was promoting and told her to come train. Money ran out. She slept in the gym, on a training room table. More than six months. All while learning to wrestle and hunt an arm on top of the kicks she had. 2019. First-round armbar. Flyweight title. Then a late round defense the same way. Six straight, all on the ground. She had become a true mixed martial artist. Paulo walked her to Mick Maynard. They signed her in October 2020.

Then she waited. Covid shut gyms down. Carlos kept training her behind closed doors, killing the lights when police drove by. Then the injuries: broken forearm, nose. Her last fight was December 2019. She debuted June 18, 2022 in the UFC. Almost three years of training through damage. In 2021 Carlos and his wife took her into their home as their fourth daughter, no more training table bed.

Austin. Decision over Jasmine Jasudavicius. Then UFC Fight Night 215. Tereza Bledá changed levels and dove for a takedown. Natalia saw it coming. Spinning back kick. Heel to the jaw. $50,000 for Performance of the Night. Only the fifth finish of that kind in UFC history. Then Victoria Leonardo in the first round. Andrea Lee. On her own birthday she beat Viviane Araujo. Still 2024 hit her in a place a loss never had. She lost her sister. Natalia said her sister was on drugs, and that you could see what the drugs did to someone you love. She called it the worst moment of her life. That fall she fought Jessica Andrade. Fight of the Night. Montreal, she beat Alexa Grasso. This January she beat Rose Namajunas. Still no loss in the UFC.

She was supposed to fight Valentina for the belt on October 3. She says she heard the champion say she did not know her, and she heard it as a slight. Her answer was simple. You will meet me in the cage. Valentina got hurt, vacated. The date stayed but the opponent changed. Her team had spent months building the camp for Valentina. On October 3 in Salt Lake City, Natalia Silva - the girl from a town of five thousand who did not know what MMA was, who raffled tickets just to reach a fight, who slept on the training room table until a coach and his wife made her their daughter - fights Wang Cong for the vacant UFC women's flyweight championship.
