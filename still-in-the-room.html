STILL IN THE ROOM

BEYOND THE ROUNDS

By JK47

Share on XShare on Facebook

They kill the lights. This is a walk out, and they won’t make him walk in the dark.

Phone flashlights and spotlights start scanning the arena. Like the authorities and civilians alike are searching for him. They don’t do this for just anybody.

Four bass thuds shake through the concrete. You don’t hear them first. You feel them in the floor, then in your chest. Cheap seats get them late. The floor gets them first.

Then the growl, clawing down the aisle. It’s dark and hell is hot. Then DMX dragging sunshine through a room that still hasn’t shown a face.

Two seconds. Three. That’s the lie. Your chest already left.

Then the camera finds him.

The cheer doesn’t start. It was already in the walls. It just didn’t have a body yet. Now it does. The floor section goes first. The upper bowl comes in late and makes it louder. People who were sitting are not sitting. For a second the building is only one sound.

The crowd is in the building. You are not. Doesn’t matter. Time goes stupid anyway.

That night is his.

What it did to the room you were sitting in is not.

You get the dark before you get the man. The TV makes the arena smaller and the speakers make it worse, which somehow makes it closer. The four thuds come through a cabinet and a floor that has never been an arena. They still land in order. Floor first. Chest second. Thought last. Somebody in your room says his name too early. Somebody else doesn’t say anything.

Sometimes you leave the chair. The legs decide. The room is already standing in you. A cup moves. A voice comes out of you that you will not remember owning. You are on your feet before you decided to stand. You are on your feet in the kind of way that makes the replay look choreographed later, like you meant it. Whatever hit, it hit in you first. The thought shows up late. Embarrassed. Asking what just happened.

Sometimes it is the other thing. No jump. Stillness. The room goes quiet in your ribs. Dark. Like the lights went down inside you before they went off on the screen. You do not cheer.

You are still.

People who do not have that night will watch the same clip and wait for you to explain why it mattered. Why you froze. They have the walkout. They do not have the hole. They look at you after the growl, waiting for the sentence that will make their watching the same as yours. You can use every word you own. The words make it smaller. They do not have the hole in them yet. So they nod. And you look a little crazy. Then you let it go.

You can rewind it years later and the body still answers. Not because you are stuck. Because some nights do not dump out. They sit. Four thuds in the dark. A growl off concrete. Or the lack of it. The room is different. You are older. The feeling is the same size.

That is why this stays.

Not because one fighter was magic. Not because one Saturday was the only Saturday. Because you have a night like that too. Maybe it was this walkout. Maybe it was a ball through somebody’s arms. Maybe it was a song you were not supposed to hear in that building.

The body already knows.
