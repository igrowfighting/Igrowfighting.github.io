WANG CONG

UFC 332 · BEYOND THE ROUNDS

By JK47

Most people in America met Wang Cong the night she walked into the Apex and knocked somebody out in 62 seconds. That was not the beginning of her story. That was the part where the rest of the world finally got forced to pay attention.

She grew up in Zhuanghe, a fishing town on the Liaoning coast outside Dalian. Her dad worked the water. Money was tight. When she was young the family got hit with illness on top of everything else. Sport was not a hobby for her. It was a way out of the village, like basketball in the hood. A way to put something back in her family’s hands. She looked at university and wasn’t sure it would ever pay for what they needed… so she bet her whole life on fighting instead.

First week of training they threw her into sparring. A boy cracked her hard enough to make her bleed. Most kids cry. Most kids quit. Not most girls, most kids. She wanted to keep going. The coach had to pull her off because she would not stop throwing. Six months later she won her first tournament.

Then she became something China already knew and America never really got told: 2013 World Wushu Championships - Sanda gold. 2014 Asian Games - gold again. 2015 Kunlun Fight 33 in Changde - she beat Valentina Shevchenko on the cards and took a title. She still calls Valentina the toughest opponent she has ever faced. 2018 Kunlun Legend of Mulan - three fights in one night. Three decisions. Tournament champion. 2019 Boxing World Championships - silver. The Olympic dream she chased for years never opened the door.

So she turned toward MMA. And her father looked her in the face and said if she did it… he would cut her off. He thought it was too brutal. He was terrified of what it would do to his daughter. She did it anyway. Her father wasn’t trying to own her life. He was trying to keep her in one piece. Wang wasn’t trying to defy her father. She was willing to risk damage for her family. The family came around. They had to. The decision was already made.

Road to UFC in Shanghai. Contract. UFC debut: 62 seconds. Welcome to the Octagon. One loss in Macau that could have broken the whole thing. Then she climbed. Brasil. Da Silva. Moura. Cortez with a point deducted and she still found a way. Now Valentina is hurt. The flyweight belt is vacant. And on October 3 in Salt Lake City, Wang Cong - the kid from a fishing village who bled in her first week and refused to stop even when her dad tried to hold her back - fights Natalia Silva for the UFC women’s flyweight championship. America is just now learning her name. China has been watching this movie for a decade.
